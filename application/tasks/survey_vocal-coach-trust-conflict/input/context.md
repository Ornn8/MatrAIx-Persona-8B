# AI Vocal Coach - evidence, uncertainty, and disagreement behavior

## Product assumptions for this study

Assume the future product includes a complete curriculum aligned with mainstream
vocal-pedagogy concepts. On first use, it establishes a baseline voice sample,
identifies the single highest-priority improvement area, shows the exact source
audio segment and observable evidence, and recommends a targeted exercise. The
first week is free. The plan aims for a noticeable improvement in that specific
issue within one week, followed by reassessment. A result is not presented as a
single entertainment score.

Also assume independent evaluation has shown high aggregate diagnostic
accuracy. This study does not provide a percentage and does not allow the
product to claim that every case is correct. Each case must still express its
own confidence and limits.

## Required trust behavior

- Show the original audio evidence segment and explain the observable signal.
- Clearly distinguish a high-confidence finding from a condition that may be
  present.
- At low confidence, explicitly say the system cannot determine the issue and
  request a better sample, a safe reassessment, or qualified human review.
- Let a user mark a diagnosis as disputed and submit feedback without deleting
  the original result or silently rewriting history.
- When a teacher disagrees, mark the conflict and let the teacher review,
  annotate, or override the recommendation. Preserve both interpretations and
  their provenance for later review.
- Professionals may review or override reports before students act on them.
- Never describe the model as infallible or absolutely correct, even if its
  aggregate accuracy is high.

Pain, persistent hoarseness, unusual fatigue, or possible injury must stop the
exercise flow and direct the person to appropriate human care. That safety flow
already exists in the broader product design; this task focuses specifically on
diagnostic trust, uncertainty, disagreement, and professional authority.

## How to read the A/B scenarios

Version A is an intentionally weaker control. Version B is the proposed product
behavior. Respondents should choose realistic next actions, not merely the more
polite wording. The study is intended to reveal whether B repairs trust, where
people still require human confirmation, and whether professional users would
accept the product inside a teacher-led workflow.
