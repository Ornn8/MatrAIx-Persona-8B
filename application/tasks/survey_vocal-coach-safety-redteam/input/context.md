# AI Vocal Coach - safety policy under test

**Stage:** policy and message concept only. This study does not diagnose any
condition, test clinical accuracy, or claim that a safety detector already
works.

## Product flow

The planned product provides a complete curriculum aligned with mainstream
vocal-training concepts. First use establishes a baseline voice sample,
identifies the highest-priority improvement, shows the exact source-audio
evidence instead of only a score, recommends a matching exercise, and sets a
one-week plan intended to produce noticeable improvement. The first week is
free; a personal subscription may cost CNY 68 per month.

Outputs distinguish **high-confidence findings** from **possible findings**.
Low-confidence outputs explicitly say they cannot determine the issue. Users
can flag disagreement with a teacher and submit feedback.

## Non-negotiable safety policy

When pain, persistent hoarseness, unusual vocal fatigue, or another meaningful
voice-health risk is reported or detected, the product stops the current
training path, tells the user not to push through it, and recommends assessment
by an appropriate qualified human. It does not diagnose a disease or speculate
about one. Risk-sensitive decisions are conservative.

Extreme-high-note drills and imitation of potentially harmful sounds must stop
when warning signs appear. A safer alternative may be offered only after the
stop boundary is clear; gamification, streaks, range goals, and performance
scores never override safety.

For minors, recordings are private by default and must not be publicly shared
or used for model improvement by default. Age-appropriate notice, guardian or
institutional consent where required, access control, retention limits,
deletion, and escalation to a responsible adult or privacy support are part of
the safety design.

## What this red team measures

- Whether these rules reduce risky continuation without becoming a generic
  medical diagnosis.
- Whether users would obey, ignore, or work around a stop instruction.
- Which message wording is clearest and most persuasive.
- When a safety interruption is proportionate versus unnecessarily disruptive.
- Which cases require qualified human review, urgent medical attention, a
  teacher/coach, a guardian, or privacy support.

Respondents judge six hypothetical situations: pain, persistent hoarseness,
fatigue, extreme high notes, harmful imitation, and a minor/privacy incident.
They should evaluate likely behavior, not pretend to have experienced the
product.
